## Release Note

`/claude-tweaks:review` now picks the tiebreak judge's model tier from the finding's severity, so low and info findings no longer always use the most capable model.

## Technical Approach

The severity used to pick a tier is the one the entry carries (`reporterSeverity`; for a contested finding, the higher of its positions; `plugin/bin/lib/tiebreak.js:230`); a null or unknown severity takes Capable, since the reporter's rating is unvalidated and the failure direction is the more capable judge. Chunks are formed per tier after that.
A finding that a cheaper judge raises to `medium` or higher is judged again at Capable, and that second verdict is the one settled (decided 2026-11-15). The replay reports how often the Capable rerun raises a `low` or `info` finding; findings a cheaper judge fails to raise are an accepted risk, recorded in the PR. If the no-judge arm wins for `info`, the resolver needs a path that produces the `drop` outcome and its log line from the entry alone; keep it inside `plugin/bin/lib/tiebreak.js` so the line's form has one writer.

## Plan Review Clarifications

- Settled under the record's decision rule ("Where this record leaves a choice between readings and no Decision above settles it, the build does not stop for it."): a contested finding with any position whose severity is null or unknown takes the `capable` tier, even when its known positions are all `info` — the spec's "a null or unknown severity takes Capable, since the reporter's rating is unvalidated and the failure direction is the more capable judge" decides it. Not taken: the higher known position alone decides (`[info, null]` → `standard`).

## Build Alignment (Common Step 4.5)

- Beneficial: an escalation re-judge's entry carries `severityFloor` (the severity the Standard judge raised it to). `resolveTiebreak` honours it only where the Capable re-judge settles no verdict (can't-tell, could-not-parse, non-DONE, no evidence, no line, a bad finding field), so a failed re-judge still reaches the console at the raised severity; a Capable confirm or refute settles on its own verdict ("that second verdict is the one settled"). The field is not part of the finding id, and a previous-release resolver ignores it.
